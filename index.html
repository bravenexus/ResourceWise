PRACTICAL NEXT STEPS

Questions to work through

↗

Use these prompts to guide conversations before investing time or materials.

  1. Find out who manages the land.Ask the City to confirm the current owner and responsible department. A map classification does not establish either.
  2. Check zoning and site constraints.Ask whether your idea can be considered under the site's zoning and any environmental or operational limits.
  3. Ask for permission and the right process.Request written confirmation of whether the activity may be considered and what application or approvals apply.
  4. Build a committed community team.Talk with neighbours and potential partners about shared goals, roles, time and ongoing stewardship.
  5. Investigate water access.Confirm whether a water source is available, who may use it and any conditions or costs.

The City’s community food-garden guidance lists written permission for at least five years, fencing, water access and committed community members as requirements when seeking City support. Ask how these apply to your site and idea.

Regeneration is a circle.

Organic waste becomes compost; compost feeds living soil; healthy soil grows food; shared food nourishes people and community. Every step can return value to the land.
